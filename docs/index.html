Why the sizes still say 2021–22: NCES's detailed major-by-major completions table (Digest Table 322.10) has a structural ~4-year publication lag — 2021–22 is still the newest finalized breakdown as of August 2026; 2023–24 exists only at the provisional, un-detailed level. But the freshest real signal — Spring 2026 enrollment — shows a sharp inflection already underway: Computer & Information Sciences enrollment fell 8.4% at four-year schools (9.3–11.2% elsewhere) for a second straight term, its lowest persistence rate of any top-10 major, while Health Professions grew 6–7% for a third straight year and Engineering also rose. That's shown as ▲/▼ badges on the category headers below — a leading indicator of where the rectangles are headed, not yet baked into their size. Sources: NCES Digest 2023, Table 322.10 · National Student Clearinghouse Research Center, Final Fall/Spring Enrollment Trends & 2026 Persistence and Retention Report.
Exposure scoring — now a real crosswalk. Every major below (bar two) is mapped to its actual closest occupation(s) in Eloundou, Manning, Mishkin & Rock's "GPTs are GPTs" dataset (OpenAI/UPenn, 2023) — the real published exposure scores, not my own estimates. Score = the average of their human-rated and GPT-4-rated "β" measure (share of the occupation's tasks meaningfully exposed), scaled to 0–10. Majors marked ● cited — 89 of 91 — link straight to the occupation(s) their graduates actually go on to do; hover any rectangle for the exact SOC code and both scores. Only Liberal Arts & General Studies and Interdisciplinary & General Studies stay as rubric estimates — genuinely too heterogeneous for one occupation to represent. A few real numbers reshuffled the picture: Animal Science jumps to 7.0 (its closest match, Animal Scientists, was human-rated surprisingly exposed — mostly research and reporting work, not just hands-on husbandry), while Fire Science and Fitness & Wellness drop to 1.0, among the lowest scores on the board. I also layered in real Anthropic Economic Index figures (actual Claude usage, not hypothetical ratings): as of Feb 2026, 49% of U.S. jobs have had Claude used for at least a quarter of their tasks (up from 36% a year earlier), and augmented use (52%) still edges out fully automated use (45%). One caveat worth carrying into Computer Science's score below: Anthropic's own data shows software developers are less affected than naive task-coverage implies, once weighted by how hard the delegated tasks actually are. Sources: Eloundou, Manning, Mishkin & Rock, "GPTs are GPTs" (arXiv:2303.10130 / Science 2024), occ_level.csv, github.com/openai/GPTs-are-GPTs · Anthropic Economic Index reports, Jan & Mar 2026.

Methodology. Degree counts: NCES Digest of Education Statistics / IPEDS, newest finalized detailed breakdown (2021–22, ~2.0M bachelor's degrees), broad categories split into majors using typical sub-field proportions. Category ▲/▼ badges: real Spring 2026 enrollment momentum from the National Student Clearinghouse — a separate, faster-moving signal layered on top, not baked into the sizes. Exposure scores: ● cited majors (89 of 91) are mapped to their actual closest occupation(s) — typically the field graduates most commonly enter — and scored using Eloundou, Manning, Mishkin & Rock's "GPTs are GPTs" (arXiv:2303.10130), the real published occupation-level dataset, averaging their human-rated and GPT-4-rated β measure and scaling to 0–10. The occupation-to-major mapping itself (which SOC code best represents each major) is my own judgment, built on standard career-pathway knowledge, not an official crosswalk — a different reasonable mapping would shift some scores. Only Liberal Arts & General Studies and Interdisciplinary & General Studies remain rubric estimates, honestly uncited, since no single occupation reasonably represents them. Anthropic Economic Index figures (real Claude.ai usage data, not hypothetical ratings) are layered in as separate context above. Treat the whole thing as an illustrative exploration, not a forecast of which majors or careers will change — the same caveat Karpathy gave his own project.
